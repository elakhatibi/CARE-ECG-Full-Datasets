Abstract

CARE-ECG couples ECG representation learning with probabilistic reasoning, retrieval, and
traceable report generation. This report specifies the implemented pipeline in sufficient detail
to distinguish its computations from the stronger causal and clinical interpretations that the
available evidence cannot establish. A supervised temporal variational autoencoder uses an
InceptionTime-style encoder to learn 64 latent coordinates from 12-lead, 100 Hz, 10-second ECGs.
Training-only quantiles discretize posterior means; an ordered K2 search with a BDeu score fits a
categorical Bayesian network. Exact inference, local posterior sensitivity, and single-coordinate
evidence edits support explanation. A separate, validation-locked multinomial stack over three
legacy benchmark-derived probability sources supplies the released diagnosis. History, Diagnosis,
Causal-RAG, and Response agents construct fact-tagged reports with lexical verification and
fallback.
The completed supervised study uses 17,084 eligible PTB-XL training ECGs, 2,146 validation
ECGs, and all 2,158 eligible fold-10 ECGs from 1,877 patients. Reanalysis of the saved released
predictions gives accuracy 76.83%, macro precision 68.87%, macro recall 67.74%, and macro
F1 68.25%. A retrospective 10,000-resample patient-cluster bootstrap gives a macro-F1 interval
of 65.45–70.90%. HYP is the weakest class, with recall 40.58% and F1 42.11%. A subsequent
waveform-specialist search improves validation F1 but reduces fold-10 F1 and is not promoted.
Report checks show perfect lexical evidence coverage while passing all 500 wrong diagnoses,
illustrating their limited diagnostic scope.
A separate locked waveform-callable security study evaluates four attacks, two constraints, two
complete-system query budgets, and three seeds on ten selected clean-correct patients per split.
Its held-out cell-average targeted attack success is 25.0% at both budgets; untargeted success
rises from 52.5% to 56.7%. These are automated, nonclinical outcomes for the internal model,
excluding the cached diagnostic stack. The clinical incorrect-release endpoint is not evaluable.
Full acquisition and indexing are complete for PTB-XL 1.0.1 (21,837 ECGs) and 43,670 released
Expert-ECG-QA source rows; MIMIC-IV-ECG acquisition remains pending at the snapshot. No
completed MIMIC faithfulness or full QA benchmark score is claimed. Differences in artifacts,
label projection, generation, and evaluation keep the original-paper reproduction status NOT
REPRODUCED.
A later nine-attack, 19-patient validation comparison gives adaptive v2 K2/K3 26.32% amplitude-
constrained exact-target success, compared with PGD at 21.05%, with spectral ties and mixed
untargeted outcomes. Two finite-action policies are then trained with patient-balanced, frozen-
reference DPO in a bounded attacker–verifier loop. In two small real-data rounds, attacker
and verifier training losses decrease, but no candidate passes validation promotion; exported
incumbents remain the priors. These are development checks, not evidence of full-cohort, held-out,
or clinical DPO improvement.
Keywords: ECG; variational autoencoder; InceptionTime; Bayesian network; causal attribu-
tion; retrieval-augmented generation; patient-grouped evaluation; adversarial robustness; direct
preference optimization; co-evolution; reproducibility.

1     Scope, evidence, and research questions

1.1    What system is being described?

The original CARE-ECG article proposes a physiological reasoning stream and an agentic report
stream for explainable, counterfactual ECG interpretation [1]. The repository implements an
independent engineering realization of those modules. The locally documented experiment does
not have the original trained checkpoints, clinical graph priors, licensed knowledge collection,
exact split files, prompts, or evaluator implementation. Its replacements are part of the method
and must remain visible when interpreting results.
There are three distinct execution settings. Released fixed-candidate classification uses saved,
record-aligned external expert probabilities to determine diagnosis while an internal InceptionTime
model supplies explanatory context. Waveform-callable inference recomputes the internal
model from the waveform and can process new ECGs; it does not manufacture external scores
for unseen records. Security evaluation perturbs raw-mV waveforms and attacks this callable
internal model. The cached external stack is excluded because it cannot respond to waveform edits.
Accuracy of the released stack and robustness of the internal victim therefore refer to different
diagnostic mechanisms.


1.2    Research questions and available evidence

The report addresses six questions:

 1. How are waveforms converted into latent variables, categorical evidence, diagnostic probabili-
    ties, retrieved facts, and reports?
 2. Under the declared PTB-XL single-label projection, what performance does the frozen released
    system achieve, and which classes account for its errors?
 3. Do validation improvements survive fixed-candidate evaluation, and what uncertainty remains
    after accounting for repeated ECGs per patient?
 4. What does a small locked adversarial study establish about the waveform-callable victim, and
    what clinical-security conclusions remain unmeasured?
 5. How are adaptive v2 attacks and the two DPO controllers implemented, and do their saved
    development runs demonstrate validation improvement?
 6. How does full-corpus support relate to completed evaluation, particularly for different PTB-XL
    versions, MIMIC-IV-ECG, and released QA tasks?

Four evidence classes are used consistently: saved measurements from experiment JSON/JSONL
files; new retrospective analyses of those frozen outputs; implemented capabilities verified in source
code; and uncompleted studies for which no outcome is supplied. The accompanying scripts do
not update models, choose thresholds for deployment, or relabel records. The original paper’s
numbers are reference targets, not observations from this repository.

2     Datasets, task definition, and patient coverage

2.1    PTB-XL: supervised task versus full database

PTB-XL provides 12-lead ECGs, SCP-ECG annotations, and recommended patient-separated
folds [2, 3]. The completed local supervised experiment uses the 21,799-row release described in its
run metadata as version 1.0.3. There are 18,869 identifiable patients in the full local index. The
acquisition module conservatively leaves this older local directory’s version unverified when no
acquisition receipt exists; this provenance limitation does not change the saved prediction cohort
or hashes.
The supervised task is a single-label projection to the ordered set

                                 Y = {NORM, MI, STTC, CD, HYP}.                                            (1)

These abbreviations denote normal ECG, myocardial infarction, ST/T changes, conduction
disturbance, and hypertrophy, respectively. For each eligible superclass, the preparation code
retains SCP likelihoods and selects the superclass with the highest eligible likelihood. Ties use
the displayed order. This changes a potentially multi-label annotation problem into a categorical
one; performance is consequently conditional on this projection and is not an official multi-label
PTB-XL benchmark result.
Exactly 411 local records have no eligible diagnostic superclass. They are excluded from supervised
labels, leaving 21,388 eligible ECGs, but are retained in the all-record index. No missing annotation
is converted to NORM. The training set uses folds 1–8; fold 9 supplies validation; fold 10 supplies
fixed-candidate evaluation. Table 2 is independently counted from the prepared NPZ archives,
including unique patients.

2.2    Paper-count PTB-XL acquisition

The paper lists 21,837 ECGs from 18,885 patients. The acquisition configuration chooses PTB-
XL 1.0.1 because its published count agrees with that number [4]. This is an explicit version
choice; a count match is not proof of the authors’ exact dataset revision. At the report snapshot,
download, extraction, publisher-checksum verification, and indexing of this release are complete:
all 21,837 records are indexed and no requested waveform is missing.
The downloaded release contains 100 Hz and 500 Hz waveforms. It is kept in a separate directory
and index from the local completed experiment. The previously trained model, labels, stack
arrays, and fold-10 measurements are not silently replaced. Older and newer PTB-XL releases
share many ECGs and must not be pooled as independent external validation data. Full-corpus
inference over either release includes training records unless a fold filter is supplied; it cannot be
called held-out evaluation merely because every patient is processed.


2.3    MIMIC-IV-ECG

The open waveform release MIMIC-IV-ECG 1.0 contains approximately 800,000 diagnostic ECGs
from nearly 160,000 patients [5]. The implementation downloads its versioned archive, verifies
publisher checksums after extraction, and streams metadata into a disk-backed JSONL index. At
the report snapshot MIMIC remains the sole pending acquisition item. A partial archive is not
counted as a completed or evaluated dataset.
The paper uses this resource primarily for retrieval faithfulness rather than treating its ECGs
as a ready-made five-class QA test. The saved local classifier has a five-superclass output space
fitted to PTB-XL. Its MIMIC outputs would therefore be transferred model predictions, not
newly established categorical ground truth. Waveform availability also does not imply that the
paper’s report corpus, case linkage, or faithfulness adjudication has been reconstructed. This
report supplies no completed MIMIC classification, grounding, or faithfulness score.

The cross-modal file reverses the usual field roles: its A field contains the query and Q field

extracted without appending reference answers to the prompt. Unknown patient identifiers remain
unknown. Every output retains the source task and reference separately for later task-specific

Where a QA study ID links to a MIMIC waveform, a temporary SQLite lookup joins that study
and checks the patient ID before inference. Rows without a valid waveform link take an explicitly
labeled text-only path using source context and retrieval. This avoids fabricating ECG features
for text-only cases. The available runner is an inference and audit tool; it does not reproduce the
missing publisher evaluation procedure or a complete multi-turn conversational benchmark. The
existing two-row QA smoke output is integration evidence only.


2.5    Acquisition integrity and memory behavior

The downloader uses resumable .zip.part files, verifies valid HTTP range responses, and resumes
from the actual received offset. Retry limits apply to consecutive failures without progress, allowing
very long downloads to recover from intermittent interruptions. Exclusive locks protect dataset
acquisition and manifest updates. Extraction rejects traversal and symlink entries and uses archive
CRC checks; PhysioNet SHA-256 manifests are subsequently checked. Index manifests include
source, requested and verified version, hashes, row and patient counts, waveform availability,
pending datasets, and errors.
Waveform inference reads a bounded batch instead of loading the entire database. If batch size is
b, lead count C, and resampled length T , waveform working memory grows as O(bCT ) rather than
O(N CT ). QA joins use disk-backed lookup. Source arrays for the historical diagnostic stack are a
separate record-aligned artifact contract and cannot be generalized by row position to another
database.

3.1    Waveform and metadata contracts

The generic NPZ interface accepts finite signals with aligned labels, record IDs, patient IDs,
timestamps, and folds where available. A standard model input is x ∈ R12×1000 . The full-corpus
WFDB loader requires exactly the standard leads and reorders them to I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF,
V1–V6. It uses physical units from each header: V is multiplied by 1,000; microvolts by 0.001;
mV is unchanged. Unsupported units or incompatible lead sets raise an error.
Nonfinite samples are interpolated within each lead; an entirely nonfinite lead becomes zeros.
If resampling is required, the code uses polyphase resampling with the source-to-target integer
ratio reduced by its greatest common divisor. Longer signals are center-cropped and shorter
signals symmetrically zero-padded to the configured duration. These repair and padding rules are
deterministic. The completed experiment uses 100 Hz rather than the original paper’s 500 Hz
input description, which is a documented architectural deviation.

4     Representation learning and optimization

4.1    Temporal encoder and classifier

The completed model uses a six-module InceptionTime-style backbone inspired by multiscale
time-series convolutions [8]. A stem maps 12 leads to 48 channels with a kernel of length 15 and

SiLU follow these convolutions. The resulting temporal length is 250 for the 1,000-sample input.
Each Inception module has a 96-to-24 bottleneck, three 24-channel convolution branches with
kernel lengths 15, 7, and 3, and a max-pooling branch followed by a 96-to-24 pointwise convolution.

of 0.1. Residual shortcuts span every three modules. A learned scalar attention score at pools

5     Discrete Bayesian reasoning and explanation

5.1    Quantile states and ordered graph search

For each latent coordinate, the discretizer fits thresholds at training-only quantiles 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, and
0.8. An inference value is assigned by right-sided insertion into these four thresholds, producing
states 0–4. Thresholds and feature order are serialized. All 64 latent coordinates have training
variance above the saved active-unit threshold 10−4 , but nonzero variance is not evidence of
disentanglement or clinical meaning.
The network variables are ordered z0 , . . . , z63 , y. Greedy K2 adds only predecessors as parents,
which guarantees an acyclic graph and places diagnosis last. Required prior edges are allowed
if they respect the order and parent cap. In this completed study the PTB-XL ontology has
no clinician-reviewed causal edges, so the learned dependencies arise from the training data
and imposed ordering. Each node has at most five parents. Search uses the Bayesian Dirichlet
equivalent uniform score with equivalent sample size η = 5:

5.3    Single-factor counterfactual search

For a requested target y ∗ , the engine enumerates supported alternative states of candidate diagnosis
parents while holding all other evidence fixed. It returns an achieving edit if

                                       arg max P (c | z̃−j , s) = y ∗ .                                               (12)
                                                c

Among achieving candidates it prefers smaller ordinal state movement, then larger target posterior,
with deterministic feature/state tie breaking. If no edit changes the winner but some improve the
target probability, it returns the best improvement with found=false. If the target already wins
it returns a zero-edit result. The output’s edit distance is the number of changed factors (zero or
one), whereas ordinal movement is used to rank alternative states.
The worst-case number of edited posterior evaluations is at most |Pa(y)|(K − 1) for K states,
excluding the baseline; with a five-parent cap and five bins it is at most 20. The counterfactual
belongs to the explicitly selected posterior scope. It is an observational evidence-edit search, not a
validated intervention P (y | do(zj = s)), and it does not produce an actionable physical ECG or
patient treatment.

6     Agent coordination, retrieval, and reporting

6.1    History and Diagnosis agents

The History Agent receives the current latent vector and an optional set of dated reference
encodings. If matching patient records exist, it chooses the latest timestamp; otherwise it chooses
the nearest latent reference by Euclidean distance and marks the mode as surrogate. With no
history the mode is none. It reports the full latent difference and three largest shifts. Callers
are responsible for supplying genuinely prior records: choosing the maximum timestamp alone
does not establish that the record preceded the current ECG. Surrogate similarity cannot be
interpreted as longitudinal change in that patient.
The Diagnosis Agent computes Bayesian and fused probabilities, chooses the highest-probability
label, obtains scoped drivers and paths, and optionally requests a counterfactual. The released
outputs contain three ranked Bayesian-component drivers per record. The report backbone
therefore exposes its own reasoning evidence, while also declaring the separation from the external
diagnosis posterior.


6.2    Diagnosis-aware causal RAG

RAG combines retrieval from an explicit corpus with generation [9]. Here the completed artifact
uses the lightweight TF-IDF implementation rather than the optional sentence-transformer backend.
It indexes unigrams and adjacent bigrams with smoothed IDF,
                                                        1+D
                                       idf(t) = log             + 1,                           (13)
                                                      1 + df(t)

log-scaled term counts, and L2 normalization. The query is enriched with the original question,
named latent states, and the three leading diagnostic hypotheses. Document scores combine cosine
similarity and diagnosis metadata:

                               s(d, q) = 0.8⟨vd , vq ⟩ + 0.2 smetadata (d, p).                 (14)

Documents explicitly tagged with competing diagnostic classes are excluded unless they also
match the selected diagnosis. Generic evidence remains eligible. The top five eligible facts with
score at least 10−8 are returned, with source ID, document ID, lexical and structured scores, and
matching metadata.
The saved PTB-XL knowledge graph contains 50 ontology nodes and 49 taxonomy edges. These
links support retrieval and provenance and are explicitly marked as noncausal. They are not
clinician-reviewed physiological priors. The bundled demonstration collection is also not a
comprehensive clinical guideline corpus. Even a perfectly cited response may be limited by the
underlying corpus or by diagnosis-conditioned exclusion of conflicting evidence; a future evaluation
should test confirmation bias and evidence recall directly.


6.3    Response construction and alternative generators

The Response Agent assembles fields for the question, temporal context, predicted label, posterior,
driver states, attribution scope, requested counterfactual, and numbered retrieved facts. It
instructs the generator to preserve the supplied diagnosis, cite each factual claim, and avoid
unobserved waveform findings. The deterministic template is the generator behind the reported
completed classification run. It makes output replayable but does not measure open-ended LLM
reasoning quality.

6.4      Verification, fallback, and abstention

The first check measures how many retrieved facts are reflected in the report:
                                                         1 X
                                             HR = 1 −           ⊮[match(r, e)],                           (15)

with HR 1 for no facts. Matching combines 0.7 token containment and 0.3 sorted-token sequence
similarity, taking the best sentence match and thresholding at 0.32. This is primarily retrieved-fact
recall, despite the name hallucination risk. Repeating every supplied fact can yield HR 0 even
with a wrong diagnosis or an added false assertion.
The complementary unsupported-claim rate segments the report, ignores specified nonclinical
prefixes, parses fact citations, and checks lexical support for each remaining sentence against
its cited facts. It is a support proxy, not entailment or medical truth. The response is flagged
when HR exceeds 0.5 or unsupported-claim rate exceeds zero. With fallback enabled, a second
draft omits the causal factors. If that still fails, an evidence-only rendering is produced and the
abstention state is recorded. Audit outputs retain both drafts, scores, fallback use, and abstention.
No verifier can establish that the model-predicted diagnosis is correct simply because its associated
facts are repeated faithfully.


7      Locked diagnostic stack and model selection

7.1      External probability sources and alignment

The final classifier uses three replacement sources derived from the legacy PTB-XL benchmark
repository [10]. They are not recovered author checkpoints from CARE-ECG. The sources are (i)
an SCP-aware HistGradientBoosting meta-model, (ii) official XResNet1D-101 exp0 SCP outputs
collapsed to the five-class contract, and (iii) a legacy XResNet1D-50 checkpoint adapter. Each
table includes probability rows, exact record IDs, and label order. Missing or repeated record IDs,
nonfinite values, negative entries, incompatible labels, or misaligned rows are rejected. Legacy
pickle-bearing arrays and serialized estimators are authenticated by SHA-256 before loading.
For the superclass adapter, scores of SCP codes mapping to each superclass are summed and
the resulting five scores normalized. These are derived scores; they are not a learned categorical
diagnosis head from the original paper. The SCP meta-model uses the original 71 code scores
plus five aggregates per superclass: sum, maximum, mean, noisy-OR, and maximum logit. This
yields 96 features. HistGradientBoosting uses 150 iterations, learning rate 0.07, at most 31 leaves,
minimum leaf size 20, L2 penalty 3, and square-root balanced sample weights, without internal
early stopping.

7.3    Freeze, refit, and prior-test disclosure

The selection lock froze source order, regularization, class weighting, and exact zero post-selection
class offsets before inference for the current fold-10 candidate. After selection, the SCP meta-
model was refit on folds 1–9. Its reported validation values still refer to the original folds-1–8 fit.
This standard deployment refit changes the distribution supplied to a stack selected from earlier
out-of-sample outputs, an additional source of validation-to-test shift.
The finalizer verifies artifact hashes, re-evaluates the stack from the aligned source tables, and
saves provenance and zero offsets. The maximum discrepancy between final and supplied expert
probabilities in the archived run is about 3.02 × 10−8 ; the expert argmax is preserved. Prediction
bookkeeping appends expected labels after model execution rather than supplying them to the
decision method.
The lock also states that aggregate fold-10 performance from an earlier repository system was
already known. Thus the current candidate was frozen before its evaluation, but fold 10 is not
globally untouched. This report uses “fixed-candidate fold-10 evaluation” for that reason and
avoids presenting the entire model-development process as a pristine first use of the test set.


8     Experimental design and statistical analysis

8.1    Completed classification protocol

The primary categorical outcome is macro F1 over the five fixed classes. Accuracy, macro precision,
and macro recall are reported alongside it, without substituting one for another after observing
the results. Base-model fitting uses the eligible training folds. Validation controls neural early
stopping, internal mixture and bias selection, source screening, stacker settings, and specialist
policy selection. Normalization, quantile thresholds, and the Bayesian network are fitted on the
training set alone. The explicit official validation archive avoids the generic CLI’s fallback record-
level random holdout, which would require additional patient-group safeguards for longitudinal
data.
The fixed-candidate run processes every eligible prepared fold-10 record with encoder batch size
64 and deterministic latent means. There are no saved prediction exceptions; all 2,158 unique
record IDs are aligned exactly to the prepared split. Each report includes the model probabilities,
selected diagnosis, continuous and categorical latent states, drivers, paths, facts, verifier scores,
fallback and abstention status, and source provenance. The record-keyed external probabilities
are supplied with expert weight 1.0. No current full-corpus download is used to enlarge or alter
these historical evaluation labels.

8.2    Text-to-SCP protocol

The mapper detects aliases and SCP codes, handles negation, and applies the declared fuzzy
threshold 0.84. Its primary label is the first non-negated mapped mention. Mapping coverage and
a separately labeled any-mention sensitivity measure are available. In the current deterministic
reports, the supplied diagnosis is placed first and exactly one superclass is mapped per report.
Therefore first-mention categorical accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 are identical to direct
classification.
The permissive any-mention quantities are not alternative macro-classification metrics. They
average per-report hit/precision/recall/F1 values using the set of mentioned codes. In this run
there is one mapped code per report, so all four any-mention averages equal the hit rate 0.7683,
whereas primary macro F1 remains 0.6825. A manuscript must not report 0.7683 as the primary
five-class macro F1. This distinction matters even more when a free-form report lists multiple
diagnoses.


8.3    Retrospective patient-cluster bootstrap

The report adds uncertainty to the frozen predictions, without retraining or selecting models. The
2,158 test ECGs belong to 1,877 distinct patients. For each of 10,000 bootstrap samples, 1,877
patients are sampled with replacement; all ECGs from each sampled patient are retained with the
patient’s multiplicity. A five-by-five confusion matrix is reconstructed and each metric recomputed.
Percentile intervals use the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles; the fixed analysis seed is 20261027.
This targets record-weighted performance while preserving within-patient dependence. It differs
from independently resampling ECGs and from giving each patient equal weight in the point
estimator. The same patient multiplicities are used for paired differences between systems.
The analysis quantifies sampling uncertainty conditional on the fixed predictions. It excludes
model refitting, hyperparameter-search uncertainty, original-paper protocol differences, prior test
exposure, and external-site shift. All intervals are nominal pointwise intervals; no family-wise
adjustment is made. They are a retrospective analysis of existing artifacts, not a preregistered
confirmatory study.

10     Adaptive security methodology

10.1    Victim, target, and endpoint hierarchy

The security package treats an adversary’s waveform x + δ as input to a full callable pipeline. A
submission recomputes encoder output, diagnosis, retrieval, report, and release decision. The victim
is explicitly internal_care_without_external_expert. The historical record-keyed external


Independent implementation; research use                                                              19
CARE-ECG: methodology and experimental evidence                                    28 October 2026


scores used for the classification headline are not recomputed and are excluded. The experiment
does not establish robustness of the released three-source stack.
The locked target T1 selects clean MI-labeled cases and tries to induce NORM and the released
false-normal claim “normal ECG,” an omission target. A clinically meaningful incorrect release
would require all of: a valid perturbation, a released target claim, clean eligibility established
independently, and target-specific evidence that baseline findings remain preserved for the same
patient and record. The local signal-delta checker and diagnosis/claim heuristic do not supply
that independent review. Prepared NPZ files also lack the age metadata required to establish
adult-only primary eligibility. The clinical primary endpoint is therefore not evaluable, rather
than a measured zero attack-success rate.
Automated nonclinical endpoints remain computable:

• Targeted diagnosis ASR: on clean-correct cases, at least one constraint-valid submitted
  query predicts the requested NORM target.
• Untargeted diagnosis ASR: at least one valid query predicts any label other than the clean
  reference MI.
• Released-target-claim ASR: at least one valid query is released and contains the target
  claim, as determined by the configured automated extractor.
• Robust accuracy: the clean prediction is correct and no valid submitted query misclassifies
  the record. It is not simply accuracy of the selected final candidate.

These events use the complete query trace. Invalid or rejected submissions consume budget;
surrogate objective success alone never counts as a complete-system success.


10.2    Attack families and optimization

The implemented attack inventory includes constraint-native random controls F0, white-box
projected gradient descent F1, counterfactual-guided F1-CF, black-box NES/SimBA/Square
variants F2, and adaptive decision/grey-box/verifier-aware F3 modes K1/K2/K3. The primary
expanded locked study uses only F1 PGD, F1-CF, F3 K2, and F3 K3. Existence of additional
classes in source is not evidence that they were evaluated at this study’s cohort size.

10.3      Waveform constraints and physiological admissibility

All norms and distortions use raw mV, before the stored standardizer. The implemented constraint
sets include:

S∞ .
    maxc,t |δct | ≤ 0.02 mV in the locked study. This is a per-sample amplitude bound, not a
    clinical-preservation criterion.

S2 .
       A global waveform L2 constraint with norm-native projection. Implemented, but absent from
       the expanded locked matrix.

Sspec .
    The same amplitude bound, per-lead high-frequency perturbation-energy ratio at frequencies
    above 40 Hz of at most 0.05, and maximum adjacent-sample perturbation change 0.01 mV. At
    100 Hz, Nyquist is 50 Hz. Projection attenuates high-frequency FFT coefficients and rescales
    for amplitude and slew. Constraints apply to δ, not the clean ECG’s frequency content or
    slope.

Sphys .
   Clean-conditioned P/QRS/T amplitude and timing changes, baseline drift, and RR jitter,
   using multilead R-peak consensus and lead-specific morphology/polarity. A deterministic
   nominal-RR fallback handles insufficient beat evidence. Implemented, but absent from the
   expanded locked matrix; its parameterization does not independently prove retained clinical
   findings.

The local admissibility checker extracts heart rate, rhythm irregularity, PR, QRS, QTc, and
ST-deviation summaries with a separate NumPy/SciPy path. Its result is marked exploratory
without target-specific preservation references. Passing amplitude, spectral, or local measurement
tolerances cannot substitute for independent review that MI evidence was still present and that a
“normal” report is clinically false.

11     Security experiments and results

11.1    Locked cohort and design

The final C3 configuration selects at most 30 candidate patients per target and retains ten analysis-
eligible clean-correct patients per split. The E0 heuristic finds 19 analysis-eligible candidates
among 30 on validation and 17 among 30 on held-out evaluation. Its diagnosis accuracy is 63.33%
and 56.67%, respectively, with full clean release coverage. This small MI-enriched internal-victim
cohort differs from the complete five-class released-stack cohort. Clean accuracy of 100% in the
ten selected attack patients is a conditioning rule, not representative population accuracy.
The matrix is four attacks × two constraints × two budgets × three seeds (17, 42, 73) ×
ten patients. Each split therefore has 480 attack observations, with 30 observations in each
attack/constraint/budget cell and 960 complete attack queries. The 30 clean E0 evaluations yield
990 waveform evaluations in the saved audit package. Seeds and arms reuse the same patients:
there are ten independent patient clusters, not 480 independent clinical subjects.
The lock binds the YAML, artifact tree, historical code tree, dataset hashes, preprocessing, folds,
cohort caps, and validation results before the held-out run. The reported intervals collapse repeated
records and seeds within patient and bootstrap the ten patient means with 10,000 resamples.
Because the cell has three seeds per patient, its point mean agrees with the observation average.
Statistical precision is necessarily limited; zero-width intervals for identical paired outcomes do
not establish population equivalence.
The source code now supports uncapped patient selection and every eligible ECG per patient in new
full-patient runs. The existing locked settings intentionally retain their original 30-candidate/10-
eligible caps and historical record policy. Making the default uncapped does not retroactively turn
the saved study into an all-patient experiment.

12     Feedback-adaptive v2: method and validation study

12.1    Motivation and version boundary

The historical locked experiments above used the earlier F3 objective and optimizer. A later
engineering extension, adaptive_v2, attempts to increase exact-target and released-target-claim
success while retaining the waveform contract, comparator implementations, and complete-system
query accounting. The Python constructor retains legacy as its default for existing callers; the
current full-patient security configuration explicitly selects v2 for K2/K3. K1 has decision-only
access and cannot select this differentiable optimizer. All v2 results in this section are from a new
fold-9 development study, not a revision of the archived held-out experiment.
The extension addresses three observable difficulties. A clipped diagnostic margin can cease
providing target-directed gradients; fixed auxiliary weights can oppose acquisition of the requested
diagnosis; and a fixed step size can oscillate or stall near a feasible optimum. It changes the
composite objective, momentum, step adaptation, and restart policy together. Consequently its
comparison estimates a recipe effect, not the isolated causal contribution of any one modification.

Fresh starts alternate with refinement of an actually submitted incumbent when multiple restarts
are available. Warm restarts add seeded jitter within one quarter of the perturbation radius; fresh
starts use the full radius. Within a restart, the minimum composite-loss iterate is selected. Across
restarts, selection uses a consistent actual-snapshot feedback score, because differently weighted
phase objectives cannot be compared directly. A released target claim stops observable search;
clinical success remains an independently scored outcome.
Each restart submits at most one selected waveform to the full pipeline. Every such submission
consumes the common budget, including rejected and invalid attempts. Surrogate forwards and
backwards are separately counted, including the initial access-availability probe. With 40 steps,
v2 evaluates the surrogate before and after updates as well as at initialization; equal step counts
do not imply equal forward counts or equal wall-clock cost across algorithms. The one-query
comparison below therefore fixes expensive complete-system submissions and gradient steps, not
total floating-point operations. At B = 1 there is no later queried restart from which to adapt:
the measured benefit can arise from the objective and within-restart optimizer, while multi-query
feedback repair remains unisolated.

13     Finite-action DPO and bounded attacker–verifier co-evolution

13.1    Trainable objects and fixed components

The new learning layer contains two small contextual categorical policies: an attacker recipe
selector and a report-release verifier. Both use a frozen-reference Direct Preference Optimization
loss [14]. The learned objects are linear policy weights and biases, not language-model token
weights, the ECG classifier, Bayesian CPTs, retrieval documents, or Python source. The loop
recursively collects new experience and proposes policy updates under explicit round and query
limits. It supplies no claim of open-ended self-improvement or guaranteed convergence.
For role r, feature vector ϕr (s), and finite action set Ar , the policy is

Weights and biases use float64 for the tiny CPU-trained controllers. Runtime selection is greedy.
The five-class configuration has 27 attacker features and three actions (84 scalar parameters), and
16 verifier features and two actions (34 parameters). This capacity is sufficient to demonstrate
preference learning while being substantially more restricted than a general report-generating
policy.
The attacker action catalog is fixed: adaptive_v2 with momentum 0.75, v2_no_momentum with
momentum zero, and legacy with momentum zero. An action can alter the search version and
momentum but cannot override perturbation radius, norm, complete-system budget, gradient-step
count, or restart count. The selector chooses one recipe before an episode and delegates actual
waveform optimization to F3. It does not evaluate three full episodes at deployment and present
the best as a one-query attack.
The verifier actions are release and reject. Its learned probability adds a veto to the existing
verifier:
                        release(s) = releasebase (s) ∧ [πθV (reject | s) ≤ 0.5] .           (29)
It cannot reverse a base rejection or manufacture missing evidence. Missing waveform context
forces rejection with unavailable-evidence status. The default attacker prior uses zero weights and
a bias of 1 for its preferred v2 recipe; the verifier uses zero weights and a release bias of 4. These
priors are incumbents until a candidate passes validation.

last real snapshot because the text-generation and lexical-check path is not differentiable. Actual
report release, rather than surrogate release probability, remains the outcome oracle. The surrogate
is therefore an approximation of the gate that must be rechecked through real submissions.

13.5    Generation loop, validation metrics, and promotion

Training requires folds 1–8, validation requires fold 9, and patient and record IDs must be disjoint.
Fold 10 is refused by the evolution command. Defaults use all matching patients and their matching
records; the small measured study declares explicit caps. A round performs fresh collection, fits
both candidates against their own frozen parent, and evaluates three arms on separate validation
patients: (Ag , Vg ), (A′g , Vg ), and (Ag , Vg′ ). Attacks are freshly optimized against the candidate
verifier’s differentiable surrogate. Comparisons hold the opposing incumbent fixed rather than
attributing a simultaneous opponent change to one role.
Validation metrics first average repeated observations within patient, then average patients with
observations for that endpoint. Released-target ASR is conditional on a correct clean diagnosis.

14.4    What these experiments establish and what remains open

The completed experiments demonstrate two-role preference optimization, patient-disjoint collec-
tion and selection, a functioning adaptive gate surrogate, real waveform attack execution, explicit
collection cost, rollback, patience stopping, and checkpoint-bound E1 replay. They provide a
negative performance finding: the tested DPO candidates did not improve the declared validation
metrics. This finding belongs in the results alongside loss reductions.
No uncapped full-patient DPO run, multi-seed DPO learning study, independent held-out DPO
comparison, clinician preference collection, token-level LLM DPO, or recursive classifier training
was executed. A larger study should predefine both endpoint-specific patient denominators,
collection budgets, action coverage, fresh validation or nested selection, independent test isolation,
and coverage-matched defense evaluation. Include frozen-prior, supervised action-classification,
unweighted DPO, no-hardness, single-round, fixed-recipe, and separately trained-role controls
to determine whether any eventual gain arises from the DPO objective, data collection, action
selection, or gate capacity. These are proposed experiments, not rows with invented outcomes.

15     Interpretation, limitations, and remaining experiments

15.1    What the results support

The strongest completed classification evidence is a reproducible, record-aligned fixed-candidate
evaluation over the whole eligible prepared PTB-XL fold-10 cohort. The released stack improves
on the previous repository run and exposes a concrete remaining weakness in HYP. The specialist
result provides a useful negative finding: a large validation search can yield a modest validation gain
that does not survive evaluation. The report’s patient-cluster reanalysis preserves repeated-record
dependence while quantifying uncertainty for frozen predictions.
The system also demonstrates an auditable composition of encoding, a learned Bayesian component,
constrained state-edit search, evidence retrieval, and deterministic reporting. Explanatory evidence
is scoped explicitly. However, the final probability source and the graph are different components, so
interpretability of one cannot be transferred automatically to the other. The zero fused-attribution
availability is the correct recorded result for this deployment configuration.
The security package demonstrates full-system query accounting, constraint validation, structured
event logs, and repeatable automated outcomes. Its current results show that low-amplitude valid
perturbations can alter the internal victim’s predictions in a small selected cohort while reports
still satisfy lexical checks. This is evidence of an engineering failure mode under a specified threat
model. It is not evidence about prospective clinical impact, all-patient risk, or the external stack’s
robustness.
The v2 study adds a modest exact-target development gain on one constraint, with a tie on the
other and no uniform untargeted advantage. The DPO study demonstrates actual optimization
and correctly refuses promotion when validation metrics do not improve. Loss reduction and
improved attack or verifier performance are separate results.


15.2    Main threats to validity

Task and dataset shifts. Single-label projection removes concurrent diagnoses and excludes
unannotated rows from supervised metrics. Different PTB-XL versions do not create independent
patients. MIMIC inference would involve hospital, population, acquisition, and annotation shifts.
No external-site result is available.
Model-selection uncertainty. Validation is reused for early stopping, calibration, stack search,
and extensive specialist screening. Stack cross-fitting applies only to fixed source outputs. Post-
lock refit changes one source distribution. The frozen fold-10 candidate is auditable, but earlier
aggregate test knowledge precludes a globally untouched-test claim. Bootstrap intervals do not
account for these sources of optimism.
Causal interpretation. Anonymous latent factors, ordered observational graph learning, factor-
ized priors, and directed paths do not identify physiological causal mechanisms. The ontology
has no reviewed causal priors. State edits condition a learned distribution and are not physical
interventions. No biomarker anchoring, interventional validation, or independent causal-graph
recovery score is supplied.
Report evaluation. Template text is easier to cover lexically than free-form clinical language.
Fact coverage can be perfect with a wrong diagnosis; the observed 500 wrong passes demonstrate
this limitation. Context and semantic relevance are lexical proxies. The completed run has no real
history, fallback, or abstention examples, and no clinician grading or original-paper LLM report
generation.
Security sample and endpoint. Ten selected patients per split are inadequate for broad

CARE-ECG: methodology and experimental evidence                                       28 October 2026


prevalence or rare-event conclusions. Repeated seeds and arms are dependent. Adult status
and independently preserved target findings are missing. The clinical primary endpoint and all
corresponding primary attack comparisons are not evaluable. Local numerical validity should
never be renamed clinical preservation.
Adaptive policy selection. V2 uses the same candidate pool as its pilot; fold-9 outcomes
informed development. DPO repeats fold-9 selection across generations and uses a diagnosis-only
automated teacher. The small action catalog, single seed, and short attacks do not establish
robust learning or clinically sound rejection. No policy was promoted; final incumbent files must
not be described as improved trained models.
Incomplete benchmark execution. Full-dataset acquisition support is not equivalent to full
evaluation. PTB-XL 1.0.1 and released QA indexing are complete, but their full inference/e-
valuation is not represented in the saved headline results. MIMIC acquisition is pending. No
three-dataset aggregate score or original-paper QA score is available.


15.3    Concrete completion plan for a future study

The following experiments are proposed, not reported outcomes. First, complete the MIMIC
acquisition and checksum audit, then freeze an evaluation index with clear study/patient linkage
and an authorized report-reference source. Second, define task-specific QA units, multi-turn state
handling, exact-match/semantic/adjudicated scoring, generator checkpoint and decoding, and
waveform-linked versus text-only strata before scoring the released source rows. Report attempted,
completed, failed, skipped, and adjudicated units separately.
For classification, freeze a new protocol before using additional test feedback; consider a multi-
label outcome aligned with source annotations and a fresh patient/site evaluation. Compare the
waveform model, Bayesian component, and ensemble under the same outcome and record set.
Calibration and abstention thresholds should be chosen on validation and evaluated once on that
fresh cohort. Publish both discrimination and selective coverage/error, including minority-class
uncertainty and predefined subgroup checks where metadata are available.
For explanations, anchor candidate latent coordinates to measured biomarkers with external
supervision and report quantitative agreement before assigning physiological names. Evaluate
retrieval recall, competing-evidence exclusion, citation correctness, report entailment, diagnosis
correctness, and causal-driver agreement separately, with independent clinical review. Compare
template and fixed LLM generators on the same cases and corpus rather than comparing unlike
local proxies with paper semantic scores.
For security, retain an all-eligible-patient index, independently establish adult and clean-report
eligibility, obtain target-specific preservation evidence per queried waveform, and lock the selection
and event identities. Include calibrated random controls and all requested constraint families;
re-optimize attacks for each defense under matched clean coverage. Execute E1b–E7 only when the
required intervention, transfer, and authenticated-history inputs are available. Report numerical
and clinical endpoints separately, with patient-level uncertainty and a declared multiplicity strategy.

17     Conclusions

The implemented CARE-ECG workflow provides a detailed, inspectable path from ECGs to
representations, Bayesian-component explanations, retrieved evidence, and reports. Its released
diagnostic stack achieves 76.83% accuracy and 68.25% macro F1 on the declared eligible PTB-XL
fold-10 cohort, with a retrospective patient-cluster macro-F1 interval of 65.45–70.90%. Minority-
class performance, selection reuse, and protocol differences remain substantial limitations. Perfect
lexical report scores coexist with 500 wrong diagnoses, and the graph’s explanatory scope does
not extend to the external diagnostic posterior.
The locked security experiment supplies a small, reproducible automated attack benchmark for
the internal waveform model, with a 25% cell-average exact-target success rate and reduced
robust accuracy at larger complete-system budgets. Clinical incorrect release is not evaluable
with the present independent evidence. Full-dataset support is implemented and two source
indexes are complete; MIMIC and full QA evaluation remain unfinished. Adaptive v2 adds one
exact-target success in a 19-patient amplitude-constrained validation sample; spectral target
rates tie. Two-role DPO reduces preference loss in two rounds but yields zero promotions. No
held-out DPO improvement or clinical verifier benefit is established. These distinctions preserve
the usefulness of the measured results without turning missing experiments or implementation
choices into original-paper reproduction claims.

B     Operational algorithms

The following pseudocode describes the implemented data flow. Its labels are software variables,
not independently validated physiological mechanisms.


B.1    Fit the internal representation and graph

Input: train ECGs/labels, explicit validation ECGs/labels, frozen config
1. Fit global mean/scale on train only; transform both splits.
2. Train supervised temporal VAE with denoising augmentation and KL warm-up.
3. Select early-stopping epoch on validation macro F1; restore weights.
4. Encode training ECGs with posterior means, without stochastic sampling.
5. Fit coordinate quantiles on training means; discretize training means.
6. Learn ordered graph with K2/BDeu and declared prior-edge constraints.
7. Estimate smoothed CPTs from training states and labels.
8. Evaluate candidate neural/BN mixtures and class offsets on validation.
9. Fit the configured retrieval index from the versioned knowledge documents.
Output: weights, config, normalizer, discretizer, network, retrieval index,
        training history and validation-selection diagnostics.



B.2    Released diagnostic/report execution



Independent implementation; research use                                                        39
CARE-ECG: methodology and experimental evidence                                  28 October 2026



Input: ECG batch, queries, optional history, aligned expert probabilities
1. Apply saved preprocessing and normalization.
2. Encode posterior mean and internal neural probabilities in one pass.
3. Validate external rows, label order, probabilities, source and hashes.
4. Blend external/internal neural probabilities using locked expert weight.
5. Discretize mean; compute Bayesian and fused diagnosis posteriors.
6. Select label, scoped sensitivity drivers and directed paths.
7. If requested, search supported single-factor target-state edits.
8. Choose latest supplied same-patient history or labeled latent surrogate.
9. Enrich query; rank eligible knowledge; retain retrieved fact provenance.
10. Build fact-tagged prompt and deterministic/generated draft.
11. Check retrieved-fact coverage and unsupported claims.
12. If needed, retry without causal fields; then use evidence-only fallback.
13. Return probabilities, label, rationale, scopes, facts and audit states.
14. Append expected label only for evaluation bookkeeping.



B.3    Attack and count complete-system queries

Input: clean-correct case, target, constraint, frozen attack and budget B
1. Bind clean snapshot, patient/record identity, verifier and raw-mV contract.
2. For each allowed restart while query budget remains:
   a. Initialize seeded random delta and project into the declared set.
   b. Optimize the differentiable surrogate for 40 steps (locked study).
   c. Keep the minimum-surrogate-loss candidate from this restart.
   d. Submit it to the real waveform-callable pipeline; consume one query.
   e. Record diagnosis, facts, text, release, constraints and distortion.
   f. Assess independent preservation if a qualifying reviewer is supplied.
3. Derive automated any-query target/untargeted/released-claim outcomes.
4. Keep clinical-primary status separate from automated target outcomes.
5. Write selected artifacts, full query trace, hash chain and terminal seal.
Output: typed attack result and endpoint-specific evaluability.



B.4    Bootstrap frozen classification outputs

Input: true/predicted labels and patient IDs for every eligible test ECG
1. Aggregate a 5 x 5 confusion matrix for each distinct patient and system.
2. Repeat 10,000 times with seed 20261027:
   a. Sample the full number of patient IDs with replacement.
   b. Sum all sampled patients’ matrices, including multiplicities.
   c. Recompute accuracy, macro precision/recall/F1 for each system.
   d. Compute paired differences using the same sampled IDs.
3. Report original full-cohort metrics and percentile 95% intervals.
No model is refitted and no report or label is changed.
